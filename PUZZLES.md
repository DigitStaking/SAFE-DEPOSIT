# SAFE DEPOSIT — 25 Puzzles, 25 Different Ideas

One puzzle per 4 rooms. 100 rooms → 25 puzzles. Demo: 20 rooms → **5 puzzles,
Tier 1 only.**

**Every survivor is behind a puzzle. 100%, no exceptions.** You never walk up to
a person and press E.

---

# THE THREE RULES

**1. If one player can solve it alone while the others watch, it is not a puzzle.**

**2. Every puzzle must cost something** — time, weight, rope, light, health, or a
body.

**3. Every one of the 25 is a different VERB.** Not a different code on the same
keypad. Twenty-five distinct things you *do*. That's what this list is for — I've
checked each against the others, and where two were cousins I cut one.

---

# DIFFICULTY BY DEPTH

| Tier | Rooms | Puzzles | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 1–20 | 1–5 | Teaches you to *talk*. One mechanism, one room apart. |
| **2** | 21–45 | 6–12 | Partial or degraded information. You can be *wrong*. |
| **3** | 46–75 | 13–20 | Physics and resources. Solving costs you loot. |
| **4** | 76–100 | 21–25 | Multi-stage, timed, or the rope itself is the mechanism. |

# TIER 1 — the demo five

### 1. Three fuses *(yours)*
Collect three parts scattered across two rooms; fitting all three restores power
**and** opens the door. The door says how many remain.
**Verb:** gather across space. **Cost:** the fuse room stays dark until you're done.

### 2. The key with the triangle *(yours)*
A lock stamped with a symbol. Another room has forty keys on a board. Your
partner has to be *told* which one.
**Verb:** describe an object precisely. **Cost:** trips — or 30 for the walkie-talkie.
*This puzzle exists to sell the walkie-talkie. Put it early.*

### 3. The ledger
Deposit boxes are identical and mostly empty. The book listing which ones hold
anything is in another room. One reads numbers aloud, the others open.
**Verb:** index lookup by voice. **Cost:** one player does nothing but read.

### 4. The light plate
A pressure plate keeps the room's only light on. Someone stands there doing
nothing while everyone else works.
**Verb:** sacrifice a body to a switch. **Cost:** a player, immobile, for two minutes.
*Somebody will step off as a joke. That is the point.*

### 5. The shutter relay
A button in room A opens a shutter in room B for eight seconds. B sprints, grabs
**one** thing, it closes.
**Verb:** remote timing under a short window. **Cost:** you will not get everything out.

---

# TIER 2 — partial information

### 6. Matched dials
Two dials, two rooms, same number at the same moment. Neither can see the other.
**Verb:** synchronise a value blind.

### 7. The burnt manifest
An unlabelled breaker panel. The labels are on a scorched sheet elsewhere, half
legible. Wrong switches kill the floor lights for 20 seconds.
**Verb:** act on incomplete information. **Cost:** light, and noise.

### 8. The cassette
An audio tape in room A; the deck is in room B. The code is spoken once, quietly,
and the tape degrades a little on every replay.
**Verb:** extract information from audio that is running out.

### 9. The blueprint
A floor plan bolted to a wall marks a hidden panel in a different room. It cannot
be carried. It has to be read, remembered, and described.
**Verb:** relay spatial information from memory.

### 10. The intercom
A panel in room A shows live instructions for a machine in room B. The reader
can't see the machine; the operator can't see the instructions.
**Verb:** talk someone through an operation in real time.

### 11. The sequence
Three valves must be opened **in an order** written in a fourth room. Wrong order
resets everything.
**Verb:** order, not just collection.

### 12. Consumed keycard
A card that opens one door and is destroyed doing it. Two doors on the floor take
it.
**Verb:** spend a single-use resource on the wrong guess.

---

# TIER 3 — solving it costs you loot

### 13. Counterweight vault
The door is held by a counterweight. Load **your own loot** onto a platform to
raise it — and you don't know what's inside.
**Verb:** gamble treasure on treasure.

### 14. The balance scale
A vault opens when two plates match within a tolerance. Two players plus cargo,
doing arithmetic under a clock.
**Verb:** split a haul into two equal masses.

### 15. Weight-locked lift
The lift rises only under a specific load — 140 to 160 kg. Two players, or one
player and exactly the right cargo.
**Verb:** *drop* things in order to go up.

### 16. Power routing
The generator has output for one door. Two are marked. You choose, and you never
learn what was behind the other.
**Verb:** commit to one of two unknowns, permanently.

### 17. The silence lock
A door that re-seals if noise passes a threshold. Walk, don't run — and your
backpack rattles. Heavier loads are louder.
**Verb:** move quietly, which means moving *empty*.

### 18. Beam routing
A light beam must be steered with movable mirrors, across two rooms, through a
doorway. Nobody can see the whole path from one place.
**Verb:** build a physical path collaboratively.

### 19. Flood level
Raise the water to float up to a high ledge. **Your loot floats too**, and drifts
out the door unless someone is posted to catch it.
**Verb:** change the room itself, and pay for it.

### 20. The hand crank
The door stays open only while someone turns a crank, audibly straining.
**Verb:** continuous physical effort by one player. **Cost:** the noise attracts things.

---

# TIER 4 — the deep ones

### 21. The swing gap
A ledge that cannot be jumped to. Someone above holds the rope at exactly the
right length while you swing across.
**Verb:** one player *is* the mechanism. **Cost:** total trust.

### 22. The counterweighted shaft
A cage in the shaft rises only if the main rope is hauled the other way.
**Everyone clipped on gets dragged.** Coordinated, or nobody moves.
**Verb:** the whole crew's position becomes the input.

### 23. The airlock
Two doors that can never be open at once. The crew has to cycle through in a
strict order — and cargo has to be handed across, because nobody can be on both
sides.
**Verb:** sequence *people and objects* through a bottleneck.

### 24. The steam chain
Pressure valves that must be balanced in sequence. Wrong order vents scalding
steam — **real damage, and there's no regeneration to get it back.**
**Verb:** a puzzle that injures you for being wrong.

### 25. The manager's keycard *(cross-run)*
Found on an early floor. Opens a vault **many rooms deeper**. It occupies a
backpack slot the whole way down, **across multiple runs**, and the demolition
may reach the vault before you do.
**Verb:** carry a bet across sessions.
*The only puzzle in the game that spans runs. Have exactly one.*

# THE SURVIVOR PUZZLES

Every survivor is behind one of the 25. But the **bomb collar** is a special
case worth building separately, as a set piece:

> A survivor is wired to a device. Finding them starts a **2-minute clock**. One
> player can see the wiring diagram in another room. Another has the cutters and
> cannot see the diagram. They have to talk, fast, under a timer, with a person's
> life on it.

Use it **once**, deep, and never in the demo. A set piece used twice is a
mechanic; used once it's a memory.

---

# WHAT THE MASTERS OF THIS DO

**We Were Here** is a four-game series built on exactly the thing you invented
when you described the walkie-talkie. Every puzzle in it hinges on one rule:
*each player holds critical information the other doesn't.* It's worth taking
their three hardest-won lessons.

### 1. The difficulty lives in the DESCRIPTION, not the logic

Their puzzles ask you to describe **abstract symbols, stained glass windows,
shapes** — things that are genuinely hard to say out loud. Not numbers.

This is the single most important thing on this page. "The code is 7-2-9-4" is
not a puzzle, it's a sentence. But *"it's like a triangle but the bottom line is
wavy, and there's a dot — no, above it"* is four people shouting for ninety
seconds, and it is funny every single time.

**Rule: never make the shared information a number.** Make it a shape, a colour
between two colours, a pattern, a sound, a texture. Your key-with-a-triangle
puzzle already gets this right — build the other twenty-four the same way.

### 2. Design so that looking at their screen doesn't help

They build puzzles assuming players **cannot** see each other's screens. Your
players will be on Discord, and some will share screens.

So: put the two halves in places that can't be on screen at once *even in
principle* — different rooms, different floors, one behind a door that closes.
If screen-sharing solves your puzzle, the puzzle is a password, not a problem.

### 3. Confusion is the content

Their design lets players get things *wrong* in interesting ways. Two people
describing the same symbol differently and confidently is better content than
either one solving it.

Which means: **give several options that are similar but not identical.** Forty
keys where six have triangles. Four valves where two are almost the same shade of
red. The near-miss is where the game lives.

## Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes

Your bomb collar is this game. Its structure is worth copying exactly: **one
player sees the device, one player has the manual, and neither can see the
other's half.** The manual player isn't stressed by the bomb — they're stressed
by their friend shouting at them. That asymmetry of *pressure* is why it works.

# PLACEMENT RULES

- **Never gate extraction.** Gate loot, gate a vault, gate a shortcut. The way
  home is always open, or a failed puzzle becomes a wipe and players stop
  experimenting.
- **Never two "somebody stand still" puzzles on one floor** — #4, #20 and #21.
  One player would spend the entire floor motionless.
- **Randomise codes, key shapes and wire colours every run.** Otherwise the first
  streamer posts the answers and all 25 die in a week.
- **A survivor's puzzle should be one tier below the room's depth.** Rescue
  should be tense, not unfair — the punishment for failing it is a person.
- **Tier 1 in the demo only.** A demo that shows your best ideas has nothing
  left to sell.

# BUILD THEM AS DATA, NOT AS 25 SCRIPTS

Underneath, these are combinations of a small kit:

**LOCKS (12):** keypad · card reader · keyed door · powered door · pressure plate ·
multi-lever · timed shutter · one-sided door · counterweight · silence threshold ·
balance scale · water level

**KEYS (8):** code found elsewhere · carried item · routed power · mass placed ·
another player's action · shop tool · environmental change · information one
player can see and another cannot

**MODIFIERS (8):** key is valuable · key is heavy · key is in a trapped room ·
lock is on a clock · hint is on another floor · lock is visible from the rope ·
key is consumed · solving it makes noise

Build the twelve locks and eight keys **once**. A puzzle then becomes a
ScriptableObject naming a lock, a key, a modifier and two room sockets.

All 25 above fit that kit — which is the test that the kit is the right one. And
puzzle 26 costs you five minutes instead of a day.
