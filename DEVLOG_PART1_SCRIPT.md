## WHY THE INTRO MOVED TO THE END

Your draft opened with *"hey I'm Marouane, a Moroccan dev, and today I want to
show you what I'm building."* That is thirteen seconds before anyone hears what
the game is.

On Shorts, Reels and TikTok the first two seconds decide everything, and
"hey I'm ___ and today I'm going to show you" is the single most scrolled-past
opening on the platform. Nobody has decided they care about you yet — they
decide that from the game.

So the game goes first. By 0:52 they have watched fifty seconds of your work,
and *that* is when your name lands and actually sticks.

**Never start with a talking head.** Frame one should be gameplay.

---

## ONE HONEST WARNING

Do not say "and you can rescue the people trapped down there" in Part 1. It is
not built yet.

Say it as **story** — "there are still people living down there" — never as a
feature. The moment you demo something that does not exist, the first comment is
someone asking to see it, and you have spent credibility you cannot buy back.

Unbuilt features are the best possible content for Part 4. Save them.

## THE SERIES FORMAT

Reuse this shape every time — same structure, new middle:

1. **Hook (5s)** — one sentence, always naming the game's premise
2. **The new thing (30s)** — what you built this week, and the *problem* it solved
3. **Show it failing first (10s)** — bugs get more engagement than polish, every time
4. **CTA (5s)** — one question, never two

Point 3 is the one most devs skip. Your broken T-pose, your arms bent backwards,
the character with no body — those are better content than the fixed version.
People stay for the fix. **Record your bugs from now on.**
